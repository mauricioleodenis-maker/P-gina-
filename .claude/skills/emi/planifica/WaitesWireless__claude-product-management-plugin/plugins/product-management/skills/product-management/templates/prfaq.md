# CUSTOMER FAQ

Straightforward questions where the details matter.

**What does it cost?**

**How does it work?**

**How is this different from [the obvious alternative]?**

**What happens to my existing [data / setup / workflow]?**

**How do I get support?**

**What do I need in order to use it?** [Prerequisites, compatibility, minimum
versions.]

**When is it available, and where?**

# INTERNAL FAQ

The honest one. These are the questions leadership will ask, and the answers
determine whether this is a real opportunity.

### Customer and market

**What exactly is the customer problem, and how do we know it is real?**

**How big is the addressable market, and how did we size it?**

**Who are the top three alternatives customers use today, and why would they switch?**

**What is the single most important customer insight behind this?**

### Business

**What are the unit economics?**

**What upfront investment is required, in money and in people?**

**When does this become profitable, and what does the model assume?**

**How does this affect our existing products and revenue? Does it cannibalize anything?**

**How will we sell it, and does the current sales motion work for it?**

### Execution

**What are the biggest technical challenges?**

**What do we need that we do not currently have, in skills, infrastructure, or partners?**

**What third parties are we dependent on, and what happens if one of them changes terms?**

**What legal, regulatory, privacy, or compliance issues apply?**

**What does support need in order to handle this, and can they staff it?**

### Risk

**What has to be true for this to succeed?** [List the assumptions. Rank them by
how much damage a wrong answer does.]

**What is the most likely reason this fails?**

**What would we see in the first 90 days that would tell us we were wrong?**

**What is the smallest version of this that would test the riskiest assumption?**

## Notes on using this

If the press release is boring to read, the product is boring. Rewrite the
product, not the press release.

If the customer quote sounds like a marketing department wrote it, the benefit is
not yet concrete. Go talk to a customer and use their words.

The internal FAQ is where the decision actually gets made. A PR/FAQ with a
polished press release and a thin internal FAQ has skipped the work.
